# Data-source register: SE Anatolia and Upper Mesopotamia Neolithic landscapes

**Scope.** Göbekli Tepe, Karahan Tepe, the wider Taş Tepeler / Şanlıurfa landscape (Germuş and Tektek limestone plateaus, Harran plain, Urfa), and the broader Upper Mesopotamia Neolithic region including Mardin/Batman Tigris-side survey areas. Neighbouring Syrian survey data are included only where they are methodological precedents or immediately adjacent landscapes.

**Research dates.** First pass and this expansion: 11 November 2026 (Europe/Amsterdam).

**Constraints observed.** No imagery or DEM downloads beyond tiny metadata checks (catalogue JSON, STAC collection metadata, official raster-name/extent endpoints). No accounts created. No spend.

**Rule used throughout.** Resolutions, licences, coverage, and access notes are taken from official or primary sources cited in each row. Anything not independently confirmed is marked **unverified**. Do not treat a search snippet as a licence. This document does not invent datasets.

**Known-site and ethics caveat.** Göbekli Tepe is a UNESCO World Heritage property in the Germuş mountains (property 126 ha; buffer 461 ha; listed coordinates N37 13 23.671, E38 55 20.51). Karahan Tepe and the other named Taş Tepeler excavations are published, permitted research sites. These places are for **validation and method-testing**, not “discovery.” This register does **not** list coordinates of unpublished or candidate sites. Any later anomaly list should be checked against published inventories first. Unpublished or poorly documented locations should not be put in a public repository. See the legal section below (Law 2863).

## Best starting points

These are the highest-value, already-verified open or free-for-research sources, in recommended order. They are enough to start work before any paid or proposal-only dataset is considered.

1. **CORONA Atlas of the Middle East (CAST, University of Arkansas)** plus **USGS EarthExplorer Declass-1**. Highest-value *open* high-resolution layer. KH-4B best official figure is 6 ft (~1.8 m) at nadir, with stereo. Official Atlas raster metadata checked on 11 November 2026 shows frames whose footprints contain Göbekli Tepe (1105-1009A009 and 1105-1009F003, 4 November 1968; 1107-2138A064, 1 August 1969) and Karahan Tepe (1105-1025A059 and 1105-1025F053, 5 November 1968; 1107-2138A066, 1 August 1969). Şanlıurfa city and Harran are also covered. This pre-dates later agricultural intensification and visitor works. Use the Atlas first (already rectified, free, no account); use EarthExplorer only for extra raw frames. Already-scanned USGS film is free after a USGS login; unscanned film is $30/frame.  
   https://corona.cast.uark.edu/atlas · https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-declassified-data-declassified-satellite-imagery-1

2. **Copernicus DEM GLO-30**, with **FABDEM v1.2** as the non-commercial bare-earth alternative. Best freely licensed ~30 m surface model (GLO-30) and a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 buildings-and-trees-removed derivative (FABDEM; Şanlıurfa is in the `N30E030–N40E040` package). Use for hillshade, sky-view factor, local relief, and landscape context. Neither will resolve individual Pre-Pottery Neolithic enclosures (often on the order of 10–30 m).  
   https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/copernicus-contributing-missions/collections-description/COP-DEM · https://data.bris.ac.uk/data/dataset/s5hqmjcdj8yo2ibzi9b4ew3sn

3. **Sentinel-2 (10 m)** and **Landsat Collection 2 (15 m pan / 30 m MS, 1970s–present)**. Best modern open optical stack: cropmarks, soilmarks, modern disturbance, large mounds, and five decades of landscape change. Not fine enough for T-pillars or small stone enclosures.  
   https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/data-collections/copernicus-sentinel-missions/sentinel-2 · https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-data-access

4. **Published site inventories, not a detector, first.** TAY Project (national settlement inventory; Göbekli Tepe is record 970), the official Taş Tepeler programme list, and Çelik’s open survey papers (which name multiple T-pillar sites in the Urfa region). Use these as the validation set before any automated search.  
   http://www.tayproject.org/ · https://tastepeler.org/ · Çelik 2014, *Documenta Praehistorica* 41: https://doi.org/10.4312/dp.40.23 · Çelik 2015, *Documenta Praehistorica* 42: https://doi.org/10.4312/dp.42.25

5. **KH-9 HEXAGON (USGS Declass-2 mapping camera and Declass-3 panoramic)** where already scanned. USGS states 2–4 ft for high-resolution panoramic film and 20–30 ft for the Declass-2 mapping camera. Coverage of Şanlıurfa should be checked in EarthExplorer before any $30 scan order. A third-party catalogue claim for one 8 October 1984 scene over Şanlıurfa/Mardin was **not independently confirmed** against USGS.  
   https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-declassified-data-declassified-satellite-imagery-3

6. **Maxar Open Data, 2023 Kahramanmaraş earthquake event** (`Kahramanmaras-turkey-earthquake-23`, CC BY-NC 4.0). Official STAC metadata checked on 11 November 2026: at least one item footprint contains Göbekli Tepe and Şanlıurfa city; additional footprints cover the city. Karahan Tepe and Harran lie inside the overall event envelope but were **not** inside any of the individual image-footprint bboxes in that collection. This is useful modern very-high-resolution context and disturbance mapping around Göbekli Tepe / Urfa city, not a regional Taş Tepeler wall-to-wall archive. Pair with Copernicus EMS EMSR648 damage products (urban, not archaeological).  
   https://maxar-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/events/catalog.json · https://www.maxar.com/open-data · https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/activations/EMSR648/

7. **Chronology and literature corpora that can be queried without new accounts:** ex oriente PPND (Göbekli Tepe has a dedicated site page), XRONOS (open radiocarbon infrastructure and JSON API; site-level completeness for Taş Tepeler was not confirmed in this pass), OpenAlex (keyless API), and the open DAI / *Neo-Lithics* / DergiPark papers listed below.

**What the data actually support as research directions** (decision still open): (a) CORONA-based detection and mapping of *tells/mounds and landscape features* on the Harran plain and Tigris-side lowlands, following a long Mesopotamian methodological tradition — with the important caveat that many Taş Tepeler monuments are limestone-hilltop enclosures and quarries, not classic settlement mounds, so tell-trained models will not transfer cleanly; (b) visual and stereo interpretation of CORONA for quarry hollows, stone extraction, enclosure outlines, and hollow ways on the Germuş–Tektek plateaus, validated only against published sites; (c) 30 m terrain and geology/soil/hydrology *context modelling* (viewshed, slope, limestone setting, drainage) rather than feature discovery; (d) modern disturbance and change (Landsat, Sentinel-2, 2023 quake VHR and EMS products). See also the communities/ML section.

## 3. Geology, soils, hydrology, landscape context

Limestone outcrops matter for Taş Tepeler: the published landscape setting of Göbekli Tepe is the Germuş (Germuşdağ) limestone upland, and Karahan Tepe sits on the Tektek plateau. Landscape-context modelling (bedrock, slope, residual soils, drainage, and viewshed) is one of the few tasks a 30 m stack can do well. ## 7. Communities, tools, and methodological precedents (including ML)

These papers and groups are the direct precedents for any later detector work. ## Not available / restricted / not applicable

- **No public LiDAR or open sub-10 m national DEM for Turkey was found.** HGM produces national elevation and ortho products and a viewer (https://atlas.harita.gov.tr/), but official product pages could not be fully loaded (TLS errors), and independent catalogues describe national grids as application-based, institution-restricted, or paid.
- **Copernicus DEM EEA-10 (10 m)** includes Turkey in the handbook’s EEA39 list but is restricted to eligible CCM users, not the general public.
- **No NAIP-like national orthophoto archive** is openly licensed for analysis. HGM Atlas can display orthophotos; bulk analytical reuse should not be assumed.
- **MTA 1:25k / 1:500k geological maps are sold**, not open. The Yerbilimleri viewer is not an open download.
- **Official Turkish soil GIS** was not verified as open.
- **Planet NICFI does not cover Turkey** (tropics 30°N–30°S only) and the original programme ended 23 January 2025.
- **Maxar / WorldView and other VHR** are not a free regional archive, except the **CC BY-NC 4.0 Open Data earthquake event**, which (verified) covers Göbekli Tepe and Şanlıurfa city footprints but **not** Karahan Tepe or Harran item footprints.
- **ESA TPM (Pléiades/SPOT) and Planet Education & Research** are proposal/application programmes (institutional / university-email, non-commercial), not anonymous open downloads. Planet E&R Basic is limited to 3,000 km²/month and excludes nonprofits and government employees. No applications were started here.
- **Google Earth and Bing Maps** are not licensed for analysis, bulk download, or training a detector.
- **TanDEM-X 12 m / 30 m** are proposal-only; TanDEM-X 90 m is scientific-use only and may not be redistributed as the DEM. Prefer Copernicus GLO-30/GLO-90.
- **Declassified U-2** is not a practical open source for Şanlıurfa (main camera typically off over Turkey).
- **APAAME** is not a SE Anatolia aerial collection (core holdings are Jordan and nearby).
- **TUES** is not a public open site-coordinate database. Public Ministry pages list or count registered sites.
- **EAMENA** is not a TAY substitute; public access hides coordinates.
- **DANE** as a named living atlas was not found. **ASPRO** is a chronological scheme, not an open GIS. **“Neolithic Settlements of the Near East”** as a distinct database was not verified.
- **Open Context, tDAR, Zenodo, and iDAI.field** did not yield a confirmed public excavation GIS or official full-site 3D model for Göbekli Tepe or Karahan Tepe in this pass. Unofficial Sketchfab models are not usable as metric ground truth.
- **Yardımcı 2004** and much of the Tigris survey record exist as **publications**, not as open GIS.
- **EU-DEM** is superseded and no longer maintained; even if Turkey is included, GLO-30 is the better open DEM.

## Legal and ethical context (Turkey)

Credible sources, not legal advice:

- **Law No. 2863** on the Conservation of Cultural and Natural Property (official text: https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/MevzuatMetin/1.5.2863.pdf). UNESCO’s Göbekli Tepe dossier also cites this law. Salient points:
  - Art. 4: anyone who finds cultural property, or learns that it exists on land they own or use, must report it within three days to the nearest museum, muhtar, or administrative authority.
  - Art. 5: protected movable and immovable cultural property is in the nature of **state property**.
  - Art. 35: the right to carry out research, sounding and excavation of cultural property belongs to the Ministry of Culture and Tourism; survey permission is issued by the Ministry, and sounding/excavation permission is issued by Presidential decision (as amended).
  - Art. 74: unauthorised excavation or sounding is a criminal offence (two to five years’ imprisonment).
- The law as read here does **not** contain an explicit ban on publishing coordinates of already-public, registered sites such as Göbekli Tepe. That is not a licence to treat unpublished survey points as public data.
- Göbekli Tepe itself is a 1st Degree Archaeological Conservation Site (2005) with a later buffer-zone registration (UNESCO listing: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1572/).
- Remote-sensing ethics: EAMENA-related work (e.g. Rayne et al., *Archaeological Prospection*, https://doi.org/10.1002/arp.1816) discusses the looting risk of releasing precise locations of previously unpublished sites, and the need to work with national authorities. Practical rule: keep unpublished or uncertain candidate coordinates **private**; use published sites only for validation; do not put candidate coordinates in a public git repo.

## Practical implications (still no need to pick a single direction)

- There **is** substantial open data. The gap is not “no imagery”; it is “no open LiDAR / no open sub-10 m DEM,” plus “no official open geological GIS,” plus “no official open 3D of the excavations.”
- A first technical milestone that fits the data and spends nothing: (1) GLO-30 or FABDEM hillshade / sky-view / local relief over the Germuş–Tektek plateau and the Harran plain; (2) a Sentinel-2 seasonal composite and a Landsat change pair; (3) CORONA Atlas frames already shown to cover Göbekli Tepe, Karahan Tepe, Şanlıurfa and Harran; (4) a **published-only** validation list from TAY, the Taş Tepeler programme list, and Çelik’s OA survey papers; (5) optional visual check of Maxar Open Data COGs over Göbekli Tepe / Urfa city (non-commercial) and of EMSR648 if the Sanliurfa AOI is confirmed.
- Do not expect a 30 m or 10 m detector to “find” T-pillar buildings. The historically valuable, legally usable high-resolution layer is **1960s–1980s declassified film**, not modern free optical satellites and not Google Earth.
- If a direction is later chosen, the data best support: **CORONA interpretation (and, cautiously, detection) of mounds and landscape features on the plains**; **visual/stereo search for quarry hollows and enclosure-scale marks on the limestone plateaus**, validated only on published sites; and **landscape-context modelling**. A tell-detector trained in Iraq or the Cholistan will need a different target definition before it is applied to Taş Tepeler.
- No spend is required for the starting points above, except KH-9 frames that USGS has not already scanned ($30/scene, only after EarthExplorer confirmation).

## Method notes

- Official collection pages, licences, and product handbooks were preferred over blogs. Secondary sites were used only as pointers, then checked. Search-engine syntheses were not treated as evidence of a dataset’s existence (this is why “DANE” and a “Neolithic Settlements of the Near East” database are marked unverified).
- CORONA Atlas coverage was verified by downloading the Atlas’s own raster-name/extent JSON (`https://corona.cast.uark.edu/corona/get_raster_names`) and testing published site coordinates against image polygons. No imagery rasters were downloaded.
- Maxar Open Data coverage was verified from the official STAC `collection.json` extent bboxes only (no COGs downloaded). Göbekli Tepe and Şanlıurfa city fall inside at least one individual footprint; Karahan Tepe and Harran do not.
- The EAMENA grid contour GeoJSON was used only to test whether the official project extent includes Şanlıurfa, not as evidence of site records.
- HGM official pages and some TAY legal/about pages could not be fully retrieved (TLS or HTTP 500). Those points remain flagged.
- Pleiades and a live NASA Earthdata policy page were blocked or challenged in the first pass; related statements stay qualified.
- XRONOS site-level coverage was not confirmed (the API path tried here returned 404). PPND’s Göbekli Tepe page was confirmed at the official URL.
- EU-DEM Turkey inclusion is inferred from official GISCO wording (EEA39 / cooperating countries) plus the standard EEA39 membership of Turkey; the Şanlıurfa tile itself was not opened.
- This document does not create accounts, place USGS scan orders, submit ESA/Planet proposals, or recommend field visits to working excavations.

## Best starting repositories

These are the most useful, verified, actively maintained (or recently updated) codebases for the directions already identified in the data register: CORONA/KH-9 processing, tell/mound detection, relief visualisation, and gazetteers.

1. **iamdonovan/spymicmac** — the closest ready-made toolkit for **KH-9 and historical film**, wrapping IGN MicMac. Directly relevant if USGS HEXAGON or other unscanned/raw film is ever used; also a model for historical-image geometry.
2. **NeoGeographyToolkit/StereoPipeline** (NASA Ames Stereo Pipeline) — mature, heavily used stereo-photogrammetry suite. The standard open tool if a CORONA or KH-9 **stereo DEM** is attempted (with a documented workflow; not archaeology-specific).
3. **horengo/Orengo_et_al_2020_PNAS** plus the other **horengo/** GEE and relief scripts — the actual code behind the published Sentinel-1/2 mound detector and related papers, including Berganzo et al. 2021 DTM preprocessing and Multi-Scale Relief Model.

Low stars, last push October 2024, no licence file.
5. **EarthObservation/RVT_py** (and the QGIS / ArcGIS Pro ports) — Relief Visualization Toolbox: sky-view factor, local relief, and related DEM visualisations. The right first toolkit for GLO-30 / FABDEM hillshade work on the Germuş–Tektek plateaus.
6. **EarthObservation/adaf** — Automated Detection of Archaeological Features, same Slovenian Earth Observation group as RVT. Designed around LiDAR-scale features; methods are relevant, but 30 m DEMs will not support the same targets.
7. **isawnyu/pleiades.datasets** and **eamena-project/eamena-data** — open gazetteer / heritage data dumps (Pleiades actively updated; EAMENA data also published on Zenodo). Not Turkish excavation GIS, but the right open place-data pattern.
8. **xronos-ch/xronos.R** (and the confirmed web app **xronos-ch/xronos.rails**) — open radiocarbon infrastructure already listed in the data register.

### Pseudo-archaeology / disputed claims to ignore (or read only as noise)

These accounts and frames dominate many Göbekli / Karahan searches. They sometimes circulate **real photographs of real announced finds**, then wrap them in lost-civilisation, Younger Dryas impact, or “secret chamber / buried civilisation” narratives. Use the ministry/Karul/Clare/BIAA sources above instead.

- **[@MEGALITHOMANIA](https://x.com/MEGALITHOMANIA)** (Hugh Newman / Megalithomania). High volume on Taş Tepeler, often first to post photos of newly announced objects, but the framing is alternative/megalithic tourism: winter-solstice “discoveries,” a Gaia episode “Taş Tepeler: Buried Civilization,” and repeated “secret chamber, 9400 BC” language (e.g. https://x.com/MEGALITHOMANIA/status/2001017893052453247, https://x.com/MEGALITHOMANIA/status/2106306259632713940). **Not an excavation project.** Do not cite as a primary source.
- **[@AndrewBCollins](https://x.com/AndrewBCollins)** (Andrew Collins), alternative author, circulating the same “Beast Rider” material with Newman (https://x.com/AndrewBCollins/status/2094205466419040762).
- **Younger Dryas impact / lost advanced civilisation claims** applied to Göbekli Tepe, Karahan Tepe, or Sayburç. Lee Clare has addressed this directly: archaeology of the Younger Dryas is “a very different story from the pseudoarchaeological narrative” (https://x.com/drleeclare/status/2089424390001692745, 17 Aug 2026, 20:49); Sayburç was not “founded by survivors of the Younger Dryas Impact” (https://x.com/drleeclare/status/2090884061447540752, 21 Aug 2026, 21:29); excavation expansion at Göbekli Tepe is part of the official Geleceğe Miras / Taş Tepeler programme, “not the personal achievement of an internet” personality (https://x.com/drleeclare/status/2088152602827452799, 14 Aug 2026, 08:35); and a short “pseudo-grifter bingo” of lost knowledge / deliberate burial / pre-flood civilisation tropes (https://x.com/drleeclare/status/2079317400713859548, 20 Jul 2026, 23:27).
- **Graham Hancock–style** and adjacent YouTube ecosystems (Bright Insight, Ethical Skeptic threads, “they have stopped excavating,” calendar-from-Pillar-43 as settled fact, quantum or “circuit” readings of the architecture). These are disputes in public media, not excavation results. Official research remains a Pre-Pottery Neolithic landscape of hunter-gatherer and early sedentary communities in the early Holocene — extraordinary architecture, not a lost industrial civilisation.

## Method notes

- GitHub: `search_repositories` via the cursor-github connector, 11 November 2026. Queries that used only `user:` / `repo:` qualifiers joined by OR were rejected by GitHub and were rerun as separate searches. Broad keyword queries returned many false positives (e.g. Adafruit “RVT,” Eclipse “Pleiades,” generic GEE tutorials); those were discarded.
- X: public `search_posts_all` and `get_users_by_usernames` only. No posts, follows, likes, or DMs. Recent excavation claims that originated in alternative accounts were kept only when a ministry, AA, or specialist source confirmed them.
- This document does not recommend field visits, downloads of restricted data, or the publication of unpublished coordinates.

**Not used, and why.**

- **HydroRIVERS and HydroLAKES.** The product pages describe HydroRIVERS as free for scientific, educational, and commercial use under the HydroSHEDS licence, and HydroLAKES as CC BY 4.0. SoilGrids is the land layer that is actually draped.
- **Nar Gölü.** Not in the open NOAA Turkey paleolimnology or speleothem folders checked on this run. It is not a marker.

## Wikidata archaeological places (snapshot 11 November 2026)

Published catalogue pins only. Queried from the public SPARQL endpoint, no account. Wikidata is CC0. The query is `P31/P279*` archaeological site, `P17` Turkey, with coordinate, inception, dissolution, time period, heritage designation, and the English Wikipedia sitelink where one exists. An item is left off this layer only when its name matches one of the 47 curated pins within 3 km, or when it sits within 150 m of a curated pin regardless of name. A different monument nearby stays. ## Atlas CORONA windows (added 11 November 2026)

No account and no payment. The atlas clips three site windows from the CAST orthorectified GeoTIFFs on `https://cast-ftp1.cast.uark.edu/coronaftp2/`. The full frames (about 500–800 MB) are not in git. The NITF scans on `coronaftp/` are the raw frames and were not used.

The site licence line is CC BY-SA 4.0. The page says reproductions should be credited to the Center for Advanced Spatial Technologies, University of Arkansas/U.S. Geological Survey. https://corona.cast.uark.edu/

| Place | Frame | Date | File used | Ground pixel |

| Karahan Tepe | 1105-1025A059 | 5 Nov 1968 | `1105-1025d/1105-1025da/ds1105-1025da059.tif` | about 1.9 m |

Each GeoTIFF is EPSG:4326 and already orthorectified by CAST. The atlas resamples that grid onto the existing Web Mercator site window. No control points were added, and the orthorectification residual was not measured. The JPEG is a 2nd–98th percentile display stretch.

Harran’s point also falls inside the envelopes of 1105-1009A010 and A011, and of 1107-2138A064 and A066, but those scans are black across the Harran window. A012 is the frame that actually shows it. USGS EarthExplorer was not used: already-scanned film there needs a login, and unscanned film is a paid order.
