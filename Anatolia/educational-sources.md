# Reading the Şanlıurfa landscape

Sources checked 12 November 2026. The six original Şanlıurfa atlas pins form the core of this guide, with Edessa and other published settlements as comparisons. The [data-source register](data-sources.md) covers remote-sensing products, environmental layers and research datasets. The links below lead to excavation reports, plans, maps, museum objects and talks. Their linked illustrations and media remain subject to the rights on each source.

## Göbekli Tepe

**Explore** the relationship between monumental buildings and daily life. The site has large round or oval buildings with carved limestone T-pillars, alongside evidence for dwellings and food preparation. Excavators' interpretations have changed as fieldwork has expanded beyond the most prominent enclosures. Compare a plan of the buildings with the distribution of cereal-processing tools. [DAI project](https://www.dainst.org/en/research/projects/goebekli-tepe/1934) · [Dietrich et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493732/)

- **Excavation history** — Lee Clare's 2015–2019 fieldwork review describes the new soundings and the re-reading of earlier records that revised the interpretation of the site. [Clare, 2020, DAI research reports](https://publications.dainst.org/journals/efb/article/view/2596)
- **Competing interpretations** — The 2012 excavation team's paper links monumental construction with communal feasting and proposes fermented drink as a possibility. The later grinding-tool analysis uses wear experiments and plant microremains to investigate cereal processing. Read the two together to see how material evidence supports claims of different strength. [Dietrich et al., 2012, Antiquity](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/role-of-cult-and-feasting-in-theemergence-of-neolithic-communities-new-evidence-from-gobekli-tepe-southeasternturkey/A1AA4FB20657599F859860D94CCD090E) · [Dietrich et al., 2019, PLOS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493732/)
- **New findings** — Gresky and Clare report the first two intact burials excavated at the site, both in PPNB contexts. They propose that sediment moving downhill may have carried some scattered bone fragments from earlier intact deposits. The study distinguishes excavated contexts from possible processes that produced them. [Gresky and Clare, 2026, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0358979&wpmobileexternal=true)
- **Maps and objects** — UNESCO's 2018 map distinguishes the inscribed property from its buffer. Use the bilingual museum catalogue, printed pages 20–29 and the object section from page 80, to connect the landscape view to the pillars, sculpture and tools. [UNESCO boundary map](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1572/maps) · [Şanlıurfa Museum Neolithic Catalogue](https://www.sanliurfa.bel.tr/uploads/2025/20250901142857-32824-28081.pdf)

## Karahan Tepe

**Explore** how the settlement's builders used limestone slopes. The project describes circular communal buildings, later rectangular communal buildings arranged on the slope, and nearby structures identified as dwellings from their construction and equipment. Grinding stones, handstones, mortars and pestles put everyday work into the picture. [Necmi Karul, Taş Tepeler field account](https://tastepeler.org/en/settlements/karahantepe)

- **A building's life cycle** — Karul's early excavation report gives the sequence of use, filling and sealing proposed for Building AB. Compare the rock-cut elements and the distribution of filling material. Karul's interpretation is specific to the contexts studied in this paper. [Karul, 2021, Turkish Journal of Archaeology and Ethnography](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/arkeolojiveetnografya/article/909296)
- **Read the plan** — Start with the field team's account of the western slope and look for benches, pillars and floor finishes in circular and rectangular spaces. Then compare the domestic tool assemblages. [Taş Tepeler site profile in Turkish](https://tastepeler.org/yerlesmeler/karahantepe) · [Museum catalogue, printed pages 30–39](https://www.sanliurfa.bel.tr/uploads/2025/20250901142857-32824-28081.pdf)
- **Hear the archaeologists** — The project events index lists Karahantepe in its *Büyük Dönüşümün Coğrafyası* webinar at approximately 58:50. A 2025 conference abstract by Isabelle Sidéra, Necmi Karul and Eylem Özdoğan describes the questions the team is investigating with bone objects at Karahantepe and Sayburç. [Taş Tepeler events](https://tastepeler.org/en/events-news) · [ICAANE conference abstract](https://frontoffice.inviteo.com/icaane2025/abstracts/export/exportAbstract-A17701IS.pdf)
- **Walk the view** — Şanlıurfa Metropolitan Municipality indexes a Karahantepe virtual tour alongside the Göbeklitepe and museum views. [Municipal 3D tours](https://www.sanliurfa.bel.tr/lang/en/kategori/9/0/3d-tours)

## Sayburç

**Explore** a carved scene where people would have encountered it. A bench in a communal building carries five figures, two humans, two leopards and a bull. The figures' orientation and poses are part of excavation director Eylem Özdoğan's case for reading the carving as a narrative. What can the gestures tell us about the scene, and what would require knowing the stories of the people who made it? [Özdoğan, 2022, Antiquity](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2022.125)

- **A settlement beyond the relief** — Özdoğan's preliminary synthesis covers the first three seasons of research and discusses the relationship of special buildings to nearby dwellings. [Özdoğan, 2024, Istanbul University publication record and abstract](https://avesis.istanbul.edu.tr/yayin/4b7a55ac-90e1-4306-9062-128ae32ee620/sayburc-a-mid-9th-millennium-bc-site-in-the-foothills-of-the-eastern-taurus-sayburc-najdisce-iz-sredine-9-tisocletja-pr-n-st-ob-vznozju-vzhodnega-taurusa)
- **Read the two hills** — The excavation team's account distinguishes the northern communal building and southern buildings, and describes Roman remains in the wider area. The project profile includes photographs, a regional setting and the team's open questions about how the settlement developed. [Özdoğan, Taş Tepeler site profile](https://tastepeler.org/en/yerlesmeler/sayburc)
- **See the visual evidence** — The 2022 article shows the bench in context and illustrates the figures. It carries CC BY 4.0. The museum catalogue uses the relief on its cover and has a section from printed page 52. Its images have separate rights. [Antiquity article with figures and credit lines](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3A35B54B3265C7224CB225FE70EBDD02/S0003598X22001259a.pdf/div-class-title-the-sayburc-reliefs-a-narrative-scene-from-the-neolithic-div.pdf) · [Şanlıurfa Museum catalogue](https://www.sanliurfa.bel.tr/uploads/2025/20250901142857-32824-28081.pdf)

## Harbetsuvan Tepesi

**Explore** what a map can tell us about a small settlement. Early excavation trenches exposed partially sunken rectangular buildings. A later geophysical study interpreted a concentration of probable rectangular rooms near the mound's centre. It found little evidence for an oversized building or large open space in the area assessed, a contrast with the excavated plans of some larger settlements. [Tada et al., 2026, Archaeological Prospection](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/arp.70037)

- **How the map was made** — Start with Figure 3 for coverage, then compare the authors' interpretation with the magnetometry and ground-penetrating radar figures. Magnetometry covered most of the mound and radar investigated selected areas accessible to the instrument. The reported mound extent is approximately 5,900 m². Figure 2 credits Google and Airbus imagery, so its rights differ from the original figures. [Tada et al., 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/arp.70037)
- **What archaeologists excavated** — The 2018 salvage report documents one of the field seasons. The finds paper describes flint and obsidian assemblages, including projectile points, scrapers and other tools. The Turkish-Japanese project overview links the excavation record to more recent work. [Çelik and Tosyagülü Çelik, 2020](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/kdeniz/article/775238) · [Çelik, Güler and Güler, 2023](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/kdeniz/article/1397800) · [Taş Tepeler site profile](https://tastepeler.org/en/yerlesmeler/harbetsuvan)
- **Discuss the evidence** — Pick one apparent room in the interpreted plan and identify what the survey recorded, what the authors infer and whether nearby excavation supports that inference. The field instrument was used directly at this published site. The atlas hillshade and satellite imagery show surface conditions. [Tada et al., methods and discussion](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/arp.70037)

## Nevalı Çori

**Explore** a village beneath the modern Atatürk reservoir. From 1983 to 1991 the rescue excavations led by Harald Hauptmann documented domestic buildings and a special building with T-shaped pillars. The modern water marks a change to the valley after the excavation. The published settlement plan and the museum display are the two ways into the archaeology here. [Taş Tepeler research history](https://tastepeler.org/en/research/sanliurfa-neolithic-research-project) · [Şanlıurfa Archaeology Museum](https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?DistId=SUM&SectionId=SUM02)

- **Read the plans** — Figure 3 in Oliver Dietrich's chapter is a layer II excavation plan that shows rectangular channel houses. Figure 4 zooms in on the special building. Compare where pillars and benches appear with how the houses divide their space. [Dietrich, 2025, Propylaeum chapter](https://books.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeum/catalog/view/1678/2865/131829)
- **Follow an object** — The national Culture Portal describes the limestone head with a snake relief and a carved fragment of a stone bowl. Their museum context explains how the objects were interpreted and displayed. [Şanlıurfa Archaeology Museum object notes](https://kulturportali.gov.tr/turkiye/sanliurfa/gezilecekyer/sanliurfa-arkeoloji-muzesi-1) · [Ministry virtual museum tour](https://sanalmuze.gov.tr/muzeler/SANLIURFA_MUZESI/)
- **Hear from the original team** — The Saxon State and University Library catalogues a German film with Hauptmann and Klaus Schmidt about the rescue excavations. The Berlin museum's 2026 exhibition texts discuss the special building and its reassembly at the Şanlıurfa Museum. [Library film record](https://katalog.slub-dresden.de/id/0-1820870553) · [Berlin museum exhibition texts](https://www.smb.museum/fileadmin/website/Presse/Pressematerial/2026/02/VAM_Gebaute_Gemeinschaft_Pressemappe/VAM_Gebaute_Gemeisnchaft_Ausstellungstexte_de_en.pdf)

## Harran

**Explore** a city through its different periods. Use the mound, city wall, castle, Ulu Cami and conical-roof houses as separate stops. The municipality describes the mound's archaeological sequence and the castle adjoining the wall. Türkiye's nomination entry for Harran and Şanlıurfa appears on the UNESCO Tentative List. [Şanlıurfa municipality on the castle and mound](https://www.sanliurfa.bel.tr/lang/en/icerik/202/7/-harran-castle-) · [UNESCO Tentative List](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1400)

- **Excavation view** — Harran University publishes the fieldwork account for 2014–2023. Use its named areas and plans to follow work on the walls, castle, mosque, market and baths through the different seasons. [Harran University excavation report](https://fef.harran.edu.tr/assets/uploads/other/files/arkeoloji/files/HARRAN_O%CC%88REN_YERI%CC%87_ARKEOLOJI%CC%87K_KAZI_C%CC%A7ALIS%CC%A7MALARI%282%29.pdf)
- **A city's working spaces** — The local archaeological volume edited by Mehmet Önal, Süheyla İrem Mutlu and Semih Mutlu includes chapters on the market bath and the eastern bazaar. The glass bracelet study looks at how finds from the excavations were made and decorated. [Harran ve Çevresi Arkeoloji](https://www.sanliurfa.bel.tr/files/1/bsb_sonra/surkav_yayinlari/55_HARRAN_VE_CEVRESI%20_ARKEOLOJI_.pdf) · [Harran excavation glass bracelets, 2013–2023](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/iuarts/article/1647875)
- **Read claims by date and source** — The national Culture Ministry's architectural guide describes more than one interpretation of the inscriptions and the site of the Ulu Cami. Use it with the later excavation report when discussing building sequence. [Culture Ministry architectural guide in Turkish](https://ekitap.ktb.gov.tr/TR-80698/harran-ve-harrandaki-mimari-eserler.html)
- **Look across the plain** — Compare the atlas's 1968 CORONA window with current imagery at the same scale. Use the historical photograph for the fields, roads and then-visible settlement form. For the imagery credit and frame details, see the [CORONA window register](data-sources.md#atlas-corona-windows-added-11-november-2026).

## Nearby comparisons

- **Edessa / Şanlıurfa** — The Haleplibahçe museum displays mosaics in situ. Its account identifies a dated Orpheus mosaic from Edessa with the name Bar Saged. The Bird Hall paper offers a separate object study of a late antique floor from the Villa of the Amazons. Compare image, inscription and archaeological context as three kinds of evidence. [Ministry mosaic museum](https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?DistId=SUM&SectionId=SUM01) · [Bird Hall study](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/egearkeoloji/article/1747138)
- **Çayönü Tepesi** — Compare domestic construction phases across the longer Neolithic sequence, including channel-plan buildings, with the Nevalı Çori layer II plan. The excavation project describes the work begun by Halet Çambel and Robert Braidwood in 1964 and the later field teams. [Excavation project history](https://cayonutepesi.org.tr/en/cayonu/tarihce) · [Nevalı Çori plan and comparison](https://books.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeum/catalog/view/1678/2865/131829)
- **Hallan Çemi** — Rosenberg and colleagues interpret evidence for settled hunter-gatherer communities in the late Younger Dryas. Their 1998 argument about pig husbandry is a chance to examine how archaeologists move from animal remains to a claim about management. [Rosenberg et al., 1998, Paléorient](https://www.persee.fr/doc/paleo_0153-9345_1998_num_24_1_4667?pageId=T1_41) · [Oxford AMS dates and sample discussion](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/enhanced/doi/10.1111/j.1475-4754.2007.00363.x/)
- **Körtik Tepe** — The project documents settlement structures, burial contexts and small objects. Read the chronology paper alongside the evidence for environmental change to see what dated samples tell us about activity on either side of the Younger Dryas–Holocene transition. [Project chronology record](https://www.exoriente.org/associated_projects/ppnd_site.php?s=81) · [Benz et al., radiocarbon methodology](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033822200047081) · [Benz et al., settlement and environmental evidence](https://ub01.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/114833)
- **Çakmaktepe, Sefertepe and Gürcütepe** — The excavation teams' pages let readers compare published examples of early settlement, regional connections and settlement in the plain. The Sefertepe project history includes Ahmet Cihat Kürkçüoğlu and the student who drew his attention to the site. [Çakmaktepe](https://tastepeler.org/en/yerlesmeler/cakmaktepe) · [Sefertepe](https://tastepeler.org/en/yerlesmeler/sefertepe) · [Gürcütepe](https://tastepeler.org/en/yerlesmeler/gurcutepe)

## Forts, crossings and cities

- **Birecik and Rumkale** — Compare Birecik Castle on a limestone height with Rumkale's walls above a confluence. Then use the salvage report to see which archaeological places lay in the wider river corridor before the dams. [Şanlıurfa Cultural Atlas on Birecik](https://www.urfakulturatlasi.com.tr/envanter/detay/birecik-kalesi) · [Gaziantep Metropolitan Municipality on Rumkale](https://gaziantep.bel.tr/en/tarihi-ve-kulturel-eserler/kaleler/rumkale) · [GAP salvage proceedings, Tilbeş project](https://www.gap.gov.tr/Yayin/Dosya/www.gap.gov.tr_115_BF7G09JW.pdf)
- **Turbessel and Ravendel** — The Çukurova field project sets Tilbaşar's medieval castle within a mound and lower towns occupied in earlier periods. The Kilis heritage directorate records Ravanda's different medieval names in Armenian, Latin and Islamic sources. Use the names to connect records that discuss the same place. [Çukurova University on Tilbaşar](https://arkeoloji.cu.edu.tr/cu/arastirma/Kaz%C4%B1%20ve%20Y%C3%BCzey%20Ara%C5%9Ft%C4%B1rmalar%C4%B1/gaziantep-tilbasar-kazilari-eski-tel-basir) · [Kepinski-Lecomte et al., Tilbeshar field report](https://www.persee.fr/doc/anata_1018-1946_1999_num_7_1_930) · [Kilis Provincial Culture and Tourism Directorate on Ravanda](https://kilis.ktb.gov.tr/TR-61662/ravanda-kalesi.html)
- **Antioch and Zeugma** — Follow a mosaic from a findspot to a museum catalogue. Princeton's Antioch project explains its excavations, the Hatay museum presents the regional collections, and Bilkent's Zeugma team describes the houses under investigation on the Euphrates. [Princeton excavation history](https://www.princeton.edu/~visres/rp/popCEA.html) · [Hatay Archaeology Museum](https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?distId=HTY&sectionId=HTY01) · [Bilkent Zeugma Archaeological Project](https://arkeo.bilkent.edu.tr/?page_id=1562)
- **Karkemish, Arslantepe and Maraş** — Compare monumental gates and sculpture, the administration of goods in a palace, and an inscribed royal monument. These sources provide different ways to study power across different parts of the region and different periods. [Bologna excavation project at Karkemish](https://disci.unibo.it/en/research/archaeological-missions/achaeological-missions-abroad/karkemish-turkey/excavations) · [Sapienza excavation synthesis at Arslantepe](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/1595716) · [Kahramanmaraş Culture Directorate on the Maraş Lion](https://kahramanmaras.ktb.gov.tr/TR-151913/maras-aslani.html)
- **Samosata and Mount Nemrut** — Put the old, inundated Samsat settlement and the modern relocated town in context. Then use the conservation programme to learn how the mountaintop monument was documented and how it relates to the wider Commagene landscape. [Adıyaman Culture Directorate on Samsat](https://adiyaman.ktb.gov.tr/TR-61341/samsat.html) · [Commagene Nemrut Conservation and Development Programme](https://nemrut.org.tr/en/about-nemrut/) · [UNESCO Mount Nemrut documents and maps](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/448/documents/)
