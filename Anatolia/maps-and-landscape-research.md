# Historical maps and landscape research

Source and catalogue review, 12 November 2026. The focus is Şanlıurfa, Harran and the Germuş–Tektek uplands, with comparisons along the Euphrates and upper Tigris. The archive links below were researched without downloading map or satellite datasets. See the [data-source register](data-sources.md) for terms and product details.

## Maps to explore

| **Göbekli Tepe property and buffer** | The official World Heritage boundary and buffer describe the protected property. Excavation plans in the publications are needed to explain the extent of archaeological work. | [UNESCO property maps](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1572/maps/) |

The atlas already offers local CORONA windows at Göbekli Tepe, Karahan Tepe and Harran. Frame dates and credits are in the [register](data-sources.md#atlas-corona-windows-added-11-november-2026). For more areas, the CAST viewer offers a way to inspect its orthorectified scenes. Look for visible changes in field divisions, settlement footprints and roads. Differences can also come from sun angle, season, film quality and alignment. Keep the image capture date separate from the occupation period on the place timeline. [CAST atlas](https://corona.cast.uark.edu/atlas) · [Casana et al., orthorectification and archaeological uses](https://intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue32/2/2.html)

For a future map overlay, record the sheet identifier, edition, scale, original datum or graticule, digitizer and rights. Fit stable, well-distributed control points, check error with independent points, and report where the sheet has no control. Keep the original document accessible so the legend, revision notes and omissions stay visible. Site plans, itinerary diagrams and regional maps belong at different viewing scales. The Di Filippo study is a concrete example of a clean sheet mosaic with substantial inherited positional error. [Di Filippo, 2023, pp. 96–98](https://iris.cnr.it/handle/20.500.14243/467483)

## Survey evidence and its denominators

- **Harran Plain, Yardımcı campaign.** The 1999 field report describes a survey of roughly 2,000 km² of the plain’s irrigation area, 207 reported settlements, and a survey-area map at 1:250,000 on printed p. 48. This is the team’s description of broad coverage. Recover the coverage map and the recording procedure before constructing a denominator, and keep the reported survey total tied to this specific campaign and its settlement definition. The 2004 bilingual monograph has a national library catalogue record. [Ministry proceedings, pp. 45–48](https://ukaas.ktb.gov.tr/Eklenti/130224%2C18arastirma2pdf.pdf?0=) · [Turkish Historical Society library catalogue](https://kutuphane.ttk.gov.tr/details?detailType=modal&id=567588&materialType=KT&query=%C3%96zkan%2C+Ay%C5%9Fe.)
- **Harran Plain, 2020 season.** Mehmet Önal, Süheyla İrem Mutlu and Semih Mutlu describe work focused around Harran and report settlement locations, sizes, extents and periods as inferred from surface finds. This is a separate field effort with a distinct collection protocol. Retain the survey year and the attribution for each phase. [Field-team preliminary report](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/kdeniz/article/1015403)
- **Tektek Mountains, 2015 and 2019.** Çelik and Bingöl describe village and rural visits and more intensive examination where chipped stone finds were concentrated. The 2019 team report documents a later season in the same broader landscape. This supports named survey episodes and selected observations. A broad area outline alone would conceal the variable effort. [Çelik and Bingöl, 2015 field season](https://www.kafkas.edu.tr/dosyalar/sobedergi/file/19/1.pdf) · [Çelik et al., 2019 field season](https://doi.org/10.17498/kdeniz.601330)
- **Lower Karababa Basin, Euphrates.** Wilkinson’s Chicago Euphrates Archaeological Survey documents intensive fieldwork in a roughly 100 km² area and reports a long sequence of settlement and land use. Its maps and method can help define a geographically independent comparison with the plain. The study area and archaeological target still need to match the question used for evaluation. [University of Chicago ISAC monograph](https://isac.uchicago.edu/publications/town-and-country-volume-1-lower-karababa-basin)
- **Upper Tigris, Cizre–Silopi.** The final Tigris–Euphrates Archaeological Reconnaissance Project survey report provides a corridor-specific comparator. Track the project window and survey method separately from Urfa fieldwork. [Algaze et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.2143/ana.38.0.2186349) · [Harvard repository record](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-e879-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b)

Record survey campaign, years, method, observed area or transects, effort where known, ground visibility, target classes, source pages and reuse terms. Mark coverage as reported, reconstructed from a figure, or unknown. Retain published catalogue points as observations and keep a separate record of survey units. A zero can represent a surveyed unit with no reported detection only when the protocol and target are documented. Deduplicate shared labels by place and evidence, and keep PPN attribution, mound morphology, excavation status and location confidence as separate fields.

## Quantitative work

The current [holdout benchmark](eval/README.md) ranks compact relief forms. Its [committed run](eval/metrics.json) recovered 7 of 47 published labels within 250 m, including 5 of 19 mound labels and 2 of 28 urban or other labels. The matched [Laplacian baseline](eval/baseline.json) recovered 4 of 47 at a similar density of 15 ranked forms per 100 km². The [Wikidata check](eval/wikidata-recall.json) scored 1,678 of 4,986 snapshot pins inside the existing evaluation windows. These are measures of published-site recovery under that search budget and distance, with different counts and coverage in each group. Many nearby labels remain poorly dated, and a spatial match alone gives no ground-truthed precision estimate.

1. **Describe the atlas catalogue.** Plot counts by period, place class and source, along with an elevation histogram against the terrain window’s area distribution. Show the number of included, unknown and unrepresented entries, and treat the histogram as a description of the observed catalogue. Label the analysis window and use area weighting when comparing terrain grid cells across latitude. A toggle between catalogue counts and surveyed-area rates is meaningful only after campaign footprints exist.
2. **Choose one measurable target.** Start with published compact tells on suitable lowland terrain, where the existing detector already measures the intended shape. Study PPN plateau sites separately through their published landscape setting and visible attributes. Menze and Ur used ASTER and CORONA for long-term settlement and anthrosol mapping. Orengo et al. tested a multisensor mound detector in Cholistan. Casana et al.’s eastern-frontier study demonstrates how a different target and different survey coverage change the resulting pattern. These precedents support methods with specified targets and coverage. [Menze and Ur, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1115472109) · [Orengo et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2005583117) · [Casana et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2023.153)
3. **Fit a simple model only where sampling is understood.** Define a survey or imagery coverage mask and sample background with the same observation opportunity as the positives. Test the sensitivity to the background choice. Use a terrain-only baseline with elevation, slope and relief before testing landform or distance-to-water measures. Treat survey access, imagery quality, present-day disturbance and modern land cover as observation variables. Limit predictors to defensible scales, label modern proxies, and report relative scores within the stated domain. Absolute presence probability requires information about sampling and detection that the current catalogue does not supply. [Phillips et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1890/07-2153.1) · [Phillips and Elith, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1890/12-1520.1)
4. **Test transfer across regions and campaigns.** Hold out a whole contiguous region or survey campaign and group duplicate places and imagery from the same frame into the same fold. Fit scaling, feature selection and thresholds using the training folds. Report recall at fixed search budgets and distance bands, evaluated area, held-out sample sizes and uncertainty by fold. Compare with a simple terrain model and a baseline sampled inside the same coverage mask. Show failure cases by class and abstain outside coverage or where the environment falls outside the training range. [Roberts et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881)
5. **Publish research at regional scale.** Start with aggregate histograms, source coverage and published-site validation. If later work can support a spatial output, choose coarse reporting units, suppress sparse cells and limit the shared output to aggregates. Keep any candidate geometries in ignored private outputs, with archaeological review required for confirmation.

The highest-value missing input is campaign-level coverage with documented effort and detection protocols for the Harran Plain and the limestone uplands. The catalogue, terrain and committed benchmarks already support an interactive comparison of known-site coverage, elevation and recovery distance. That comparison gives a measurable starting point while research on survey footprints continues.
